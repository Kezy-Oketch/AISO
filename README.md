# African Information Systems Observatory (AISO)

The **African Information Systems Observatory (AISO)** is an open scholarly infrastructure for documenting, understanding, and advancing Information Systems research from and about Africa.

> **Africa should not only be a place where Information Systems theories are tested. It should also be a place from which Information Systems theories originate.**

## About AISO

Information Systems scholarship concerning Africa is distributed across journals, conferences, institutions, disciplines, countries, and research communities. This fragmentation makes it difficult to understand the intellectual landscape of the field.

AISO is being developed to make this scholarship visible, cumulative, connected, and discoverable.

The Observatory documents relationships among:

- publications
- scholars
- institutions
- countries
- digital phenomena
- technologies
- theories and constructs
- methodologies
- datasets
- languages

## Research Framework

AISO distinguishes three dimensions of the geography of Information Systems knowledge.

### Geography of Evidence

Where are the phenomena and empirical observations underlying IS research located?

### Geography of Knowledge Production

Where are the scholars and institutions producing that knowledge located?

### Geography of Theorization

From which empirical and contextual settings do constructs, mechanisms, boundary conditions, and theoretical explanations emerge?

These dimensions are coded separately rather than treating "African Information Systems research" as a single category.

## AISO Core Collection

The first major AISO initiative is the construction of the **AISO Core Collection**, a reproducibly assembled corpus of Information Systems scholarship substantively involving African contexts.

The initial coverage period is **2000–2026**.

Candidate publications are discovered through multiple complementary sources, including:

- premier and broader Information Systems outlets
- Africa-centered Information Systems outlets
- ICT4D and digital-development outlets
- major Information Systems conferences
- African scholarly conferences
- bibliographic databases
- citation chaining
- existing literature reviews
- community and expert validation

All publications undergo explicit eligibility screening before entering the Core Collection.

## Current Status

**AISO v0.1 — Research Infrastructure Development**

Current activities include:

1. developing and validating the AISO inclusion protocol;
2. constructing the candidate publication corpus;
3. developing the metadata and theoretical coding framework;
4. piloting independent human coding;
5. evaluating coding reliability; and
6. developing the initial Observatory infrastructure.

The current repository should therefore be treated as an evolving research resource rather than a complete representation of African Information Systems scholarship.

## Methodological Principles

AISO is guided by several principles.

**Transparency** — Inclusion criteria, search procedures, classifications, and methodological decisions should be documented.

**Reproducibility** — Corpus construction and analysis should be reproducible wherever licensing and database restrictions permit.

**Continental diversity** — Africa is not treated as a homogeneous empirical context.

**Intellectual pluralism** — AISO does not prescribe a single legitimate theory, methodology, or definition of African IS scholarship.

**Community correction** — The infrastructure should eventually allow scholars to identify missing work, correct metadata, and challenge classifications.

**Methodological independence** — AISO's methods should permit findings that contradict the expectations of its founders.
